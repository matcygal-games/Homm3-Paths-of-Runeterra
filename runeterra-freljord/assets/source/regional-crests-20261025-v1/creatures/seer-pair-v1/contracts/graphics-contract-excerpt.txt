3. REGIONAL ART THROUGH ORIGINAL HEROES III

The town must look like a professionally made addition to ORIGINAL Heroes III. Runeterra supplies subjects, geography, institutions, anatomy, equipment and culture. Original HoMM3 supplies rendering language, scale, camera, light, contrast, material treatment, shadows, animation and interface conventions.

Extract and inspect original assets at native resolution. Use Castle as a shared interface/rendering reference and relevant original towns/creatures as material, scale and movement controls. Measure actual canvases, offsets, native crops, ground contact, palette/alpha/shadows and action semantics. Do not copy an original skyline, troop frames or seven-tier tactical progression.

Inspect official LoR full illustrations and relevant League environmental art. Create a reference board and regional palette/material specification: dominant/secondary colours, limited accents/emissives, masonry/wood/metal/cloth, architecture, terrain, atmosphere, heraldry and variation among troop families. Pin retrieved LoR set/version, card identity and observed cues; do not rely on a moving latest URL alone.

Derive the colour scheme from regional evidence, not a recolour of Demacia. Shared accents should unite town, units and champions while keeping armour, skin, cloth and silhouettes readable. Saturated chemtech or magical light belongs where regionally supported, with restrained native-size highlights and clear material values.

Use richly textured painted scenery and painted sprites matching the compact, prerendered APPEARANCE of original HoMM3 creatures. This describes the target pixels, not permission to use a 3D production pipeline. Avoid splash-art framing, anime, exaggerated mobile-game proportions, plastic/glossy 3D, flat vector shapes, uniform coloured cutouts and arbitrary retro filters. Downsampling, pixelating or filtering incompatible art does not make it HoMM3-compatible.

ALL-GRAPHICS PRODUCTION RULE: Do not use Blender or equivalent 3D scene/model/rig/render production for creatures, champions, portraits, buildings, town backgrounds, construction layers, adventure objects, terrain, siege assets, puzzles, UI, icons, projectiles or magical effects. Do not replace it with code-drawn primitive figures, geometric building kits, vector cutouts or automated paint filters presented as final artwork. Code may perform technical conversion, cropping, palette/alpha processing, fixed roots, masks, frame assembly and validation; artistic content must come from coherent painted 2D sources. Use the image-generation skill when generating or creatively editing bitmap art. RUNETERRA_CREATURE_ART_POLICY.txt retains the more specific creature provenance rules and the user's rejection of Targon's old creature appearance. Existing unrelated assets are not automatically approved or rejected by their age: inspect their actual pixels against this contract, preserve their history, and replace incompatible selections.

Every art request, worker assignment and generation prompt must include the actual native HoMM3 reference, output category and native dimensions, regional palette/materials, camera/light/ground contact, and explicit prohibited styles. Use one art specification across all categories. LoR illustrations and fan art are subject/design inspiration, not the final rendering style or permission to bundle those images.

Apply the following category requirements together:

- Towns/buildings/terrain: richly textured painted materials, atmospheric depth, shared perspective and lighting, integrated foundations and contact shadows. Compose the complete scene before splitting layers; inspect every construction state in context. No isolated catalogue tiles or equally sized buildings pasted onto a background.
- Creatures/champions/portraits: compact believable anatomy, equipment and readable faces, restrained highlights, native HoMM3 portrait treatment and scenery. Maintain the same character, proportions, detail level and materials across portraits, adventure sprites and battle frames. No modern splash-art portraits or shiny figurines alongside painted troops.
- Siege/adventure objects: match the original tile/hex scale, viewpoint, depth, footprints, shadows, occlusion and ground contact. Walls, gates and damaged states must belong to the town's architecture. Do not let objects float or drift into a canvas corner.
- UI/icons/puzzles/effects: retain original stone, parchment, metal, frames and icon readability. Magic must read clearly at native size without overwhelming the game with bloom, neon haze or modern interface styling. Regional emissive accents are small, justified details within the original game's contrast range.

VISUAL ACCEPTANCE IS REQUIRED, NOT A STYLE LABEL: For each category save a native-size comparison with extracted original assets, the asset/version hashes, reviewer findings and concrete corrections. Inspect both individual assets and their assembled town/battle/adventure/interface scenes in game. Check both creature facings and real motion for stable roots, scale, cadence and shadows. A static sheet, large preview, filename, generated description, provenance flag, numerical colour check or clean loader cannot pass visual acceptance. Record pending native observations honestly. Reject and repair visible mismatches before final selection/release; do not weaken the visual gate or call an incompatible asset complete because its files compile.

Compare every category with original assets and neighboring Runeterra factions side by side at native size. Inspect pixels and motion. An attractive enlarged concept, palette check or successful PNG conversion is insufficient.
